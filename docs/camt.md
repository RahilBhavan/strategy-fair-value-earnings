# CAMT research and model boundary

Reviewed against primary IRS/Treasury guidance as of 2026-10-21. This is a sensitivity model, not a tax return or an assessment of actual tax due.

[IRS Notice 2025-49, section 5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-49.pdf) allows an FVI exclusion option for qualifying items measured at fair value for financial reporting but not marked to market for regular tax, including digital assets. This excludes qualifying unrealized gains AND losses from AFSI. Application requires a return statement and has timing, consistency and cessation rules; it is not an automatic exemption for every holder. Dispositions and later adjustment events can bring amounts into AFSI using CAMT basis. The module assumes eligible assets, an effective option from 2025, and no later cessation. It approximates disposition effects using proceeds minus original cost removed, not a complete CAMT basis ledger. It does not demonstrate that return-election requirements were satisfied.

[IRS Notice 2025-27, section 2](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-26_IRB) explains the general test: average annual AFSI exceeding $1 billion over a prior three-year test period, with a test year ending after 2021. Once applicable, status generally persists subject to exceptions. S corporations, RICs and REITs are excluded; foreign-parented groups have additional rules. AFSI is adjusted financial-statement income, not raw GAAP net income, fair-value gain alone or ordinary taxable income. Actual CAMT is the excess of tentative minimum tax over regular tax plus section 59A tax. Tentative tax uses 15% of adjusted income less CAMT foreign-tax credits. Safe-harbor testing is distinct from the statutory threshold and not implemented here.

[Notice 2026-7](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-11_IRB) was also checked. It modifies regulatory-asset/repair guidance and addresses other adjustments, including domestic research costs; it does not withdraw section 5's digital-asset option. The search of current primary guidance found no replacement of that option by the cutoff. This is a bounded research check, not continuous legal monitoring.

Strategy's [June 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1050446/000105044626000044/mstr-20260630.htm) states its plan to exclude unrealized bitcoin gains/losses and its expectation not to become subject to CAMT because of those unrealized gains. That statement is evidence of company intent, not access to its filed tax elections.

`interim_fvi` is the current-guidance conditional model. `proposed_2024` is a counterfactual based on [2024 proposed regulations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/09/13/2024-20089/corporate-alternative-minimum-tax), including a four-year adoption-effect spread, without using the later FVI option. `statute_only` is a deliberately simplified no-exclusion sensitivity. Neither alternative represents a prediction that relief will be repealed.

Implementation limitations: pre-tax GAAP income approximates AFSI; state/deferred-tax details, group aggregation, foreign income, depreciation, pensions and many other adjustments are omitted. FSNOL use is capped at 80% in the toy carryforward; applicability disregards that deduction. Historical data begin in 2019. Q2 2026's sourced sale adjustment is included; later unmodeled sales/basis adjustments are not. `camt` result keys mean simplified tentative tax assuming zero regular tax, zero section 59A tax and zero foreign-tax credits. They are not cash-tax forecasts. Even a large GAAP unrealized gain does not by itself establish CAMT liability.

The publication review also checked the [IRS Form 4626 instructions, FSNOL section](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4626): loss carryforwards are consumed by positive-year AFSI even if the corporation was not applicable that year. The model already followed that rule; a dedicated regression now protects it. These browser-checked instructions are additional review evidence, not a newly archived financial input. GAAP deferred-tax expense in `earnings.py` remains a separate proxy from AFSI, FSNOLs and tentative minimum tax in `camt.py`; actual current tax on sales is not computed.
